📖 Reading Passage

The Decline of the Maya Civilization

The ancient Maya civilization, which flourished in present-day Mexico and Central America from about 2000 BCE to 1500 CE, experienced a mysterious decline around 900 CE. For many years, scholars have proposed various theories to explain this collapse.

One prominent theory suggests that environmental degradation played a crucial role. The Maya practiced intensive agriculture to support their large population. They cleared vast areas of forest to create farmland, which led to soil erosion and depletion. As the soil became less fertile, crop yields decreased, leading to food shortages.

Another factor was prolonged drought. Climate studies indicate that the region experienced several severe droughts during the ninth century. These droughts would have reduced water supplies needed for drinking and irrigation, putting additional stress on an already struggling agricultural system.

Finally, some researchers point to warfare between city-states. As resources became scarce, competition intensified, leading to increased conflict. This warfare would have disrupted trade routes and further weakened the civilization's ability to cope with other challenges.

🎧 Lecture Transcript

Professor: While the traditional theories about the Maya collapse have merit, recent archaeological evidence suggests a more complex picture. Let me address each of the points from your reading.

First, regarding environmental degradation: new evidence shows that the Maya actually developed sophisticated sustainable farming techniques. They created terraced fields on hillsides to prevent erosion and used chinampas, or raised field systems, in wetland areas. These methods allowed them to maintain soil fertility for centuries. So while some deforestation occurred, it wasn't as devastating as previously thought.

Second, about the drought theory: yes, there were droughts, but they weren't uniform across the entire Maya region. Some areas actually received adequate rainfall during this period. Yet these areas also experienced decline. This suggests that drought alone cannot explain the collapse. Additionally, the Maya had extensive reservoir systems to store water, which should have helped them weather dry periods.

Finally, concerning warfare: while conflict did increase, recent evidence indicates that much of this warfare was ritualistic rather than aimed at conquest or resource acquisition. The scale of violence was exaggerated in earlier interpretations. Many cities show no signs of destruction by fire or invasion. Instead, the evidence points to a gradual abandonment rather than sudden collapse due to war.

The reality is likely a combination of factors, including political instability, changes in trade patterns, and social upheaval, rather than the simple environmental explanations presented in your reading.

Professor Martinez

This week, we're discussing the impact of artificial intelligence on the job market. Some argue that AI will eliminate millions of jobs, while others believe it will create new opportunities. What is your perspective? Will AI primarily destroy jobs, create jobs, or transform existing roles? Please explain your reasoning with specific examples.

Sarah Chen

I believe AI will primarily destroy jobs, especially in sectors like manufacturing, customer service, and even some white-collar positions like accounting and legal research. We're already seeing self-checkout kiosks replace cashiers, and chatbots handle customer inquiries. The pace of change is too fast for workers to retrain. History shows that technological revolutions always cause temporary unemployment, but this time feels different because AI can learn and adapt.

Marcus Johnson

I disagree with Sarah. AI will create more jobs than it destroys. Every major technological shift—from the Industrial Revolution to the internet—initially caused fear but ultimately led to net job growth. AI will create entirely new categories of work: AI trainers, ethics specialists, human-AI collaboration managers. Plus, by automating routine tasks, AI will free humans to focus on creative, strategic, and interpersonal work that machines cannot replicate. The key is investing in education and retraining programs.
