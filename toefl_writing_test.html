📖 Reading Passage

The Decline of the Maya Civilization

The collapse of the Classic Maya civilization in the 9th century AD has long puzzled archaeologists. Several theories have been proposed to explain why this advanced society suddenly abandoned its great cities in the southern lowlands.

First Theory: Many researchers believe that severe droughts were the primary cause. Geological evidence from lake sediments suggests prolonged periods of little rainfall during the collapse. Without sufficient water, agriculture would have failed, leading to famine and social unrest.

Second Theory: Some scholars argue that overpopulation and environmental degradation played a key role. As the population grew, the Maya cleared vast forests for farming. This deforestation likely led to soil erosion and decreased agricultural productivity.

Third Theory: Another theory suggests constant warfare between city-states caused the collapse. Evidence of fortifications and violent destruction indicates competition for resources led to endless conflicts, destabilizing the political structure.

🎧 Lecture Transcript

Professor: While the reading offers three plausible theories, recent findings suggest the reality was more complex.

On Drought: While dry periods occurred, the Maya had sophisticated water storage systems—reservoirs and canals that could sustain them through moderate droughts. The collapse happened in areas where water management was effective, suggesting drought alone wasn't enough.

On Overpopulation: Recent soil studies show the Maya used sustainable farming like terracing and crop rotation. Some areas show intensive agriculture that remained productive for centuries. Environmental damage was likely localized, not widespread.

On Warfare: Warfare was constant throughout Maya history, not just during the collapse. If war were the main cause, collapse would have occurred earlier. Trade networks remained active during decline, suggesting political instability rather than total war was the issue.

✏️ Prompt

Summarize the points made in the lecture, explaining how they cast doubt on the specific points in the reading passage.

Recommended: 150-225 words | Time: 20 minutes

Task 2: Academic Discussion

👨‍🏫 Professor's Question

Dr. Gupta: This week we're discussing AI's impact on employment. Some experts argue AI will eliminate millions of jobs, causing high unemployment. Others believe AI will create new jobs and boost productivity. What is your opinion? Will AI have a net positive or negative effect on future employment?

👤 Claire

I think the impact will be largely negative. Automation already replaces workers in manufacturing and customer service. Even skilled jobs like accounting are at risk. I worry new jobs will require skills displaced workers lack, leading to long-term structural unemployment.

👤 Raj

I disagree. Historically, technology created more jobs than it destroyed. Computers eliminated clerical work but created the tech industry. AI will handle repetitive tasks, freeing humans for creative work. We'll see booms in industries we can't imagine yet.
