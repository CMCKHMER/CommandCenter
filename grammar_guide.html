Parts of Speech

1. Nouns

Nouns are words that name people, places, things, or ideas.

Types of Nouns:

  • Common nouns: general names (city, dog, teacher)
  • Proper nouns: specific names (Paris, Rover, Mr. Smith) - always capitalized
  • Concrete nouns: things you can perceive with senses (book, music)
  • Abstract nouns: ideas or concepts (freedom, love, happiness)
❌ Incorrect: i live in paris and my dog rover loves it.
✅ Correct: I live in Paris and my dog Rover loves it.

2. Verbs

Verbs express actions, occurrences, or states of being.

Key Points:

  • Action verbs show what someone or something does (run, write, think)
  • Linking verbs connect subjects to descriptions (is, seem, become)
  • Helping verbs assist main verbs (have, will, can, must)
✅ Examples: She runs every morning. (action)
He is a teacher. (linking)
They will study tonight. (helping + main)

3. Adjectives & Adverbs

Adjectives describe nouns; adverbs describe verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs.

Quick Tip:

Many adverbs end in "-ly", but not all! (fast, hard, well are adverbs without -ly)

✅ Examples: The quick brown fox (adjective describing "fox")
She runs quickly. (adverb describing "runs")
He is extremely talented. (adverb describing adjective "talented")

4. Pronouns

Pronouns replace nouns to avoid repetition.

Common Pronoun Types:

  • Subject: I, you, he, she, it, we, they
  • Object: me, you, him, her, it, us, them
  • Possessive: my/mine, your/yours, his, her/hers, its, our/ours, their/theirs
❌ Incorrect: Me and him went to the store.
✅ Correct: He and I went to the store.

Sentence Structure

Basic Sentence Patterns

Every complete sentence needs a subject and a predicate (verb).

Four Main Types:

  • Simple: One independent clause (The dog barked.)
  • Compound: Two independent clauses joined by conjunction (The dog barked, and the cat ran.)
  • Complex: One independent + one dependent clause (When the dog barked, the cat ran.)
  • Compound-Complex: Two+ independent + one+ dependent clauses

Subject-Verb Agreement

The subject and verb must agree in number (singular/plural).

❌ Incorrect: The list of items are on the table.
Each of the students have a book.
✅ Correct: The list of items is on the table. (subject = "list", singular)
Each of the students has a book. (subject = "Each", singular)

Tricky Cases:

  • Words like "each," "everyone," "nobody" are singular
  • Ignore phrases between subject and verb ("of items," "of the students")
  • "Either/or" and "neither/nor" agree with the closer subject

Parallel Structure

Items in a list or comparison should use the same grammatical form.

❌ Incorrect: She likes hiking, swimming, and to ride bikes.
The project was challenging but rewarding experience.
✅ Correct: She likes hiking, swimming, and riding bikes. (all -ing forms)
The project was challenging but was a rewarding experience. (parallel clauses)

Punctuation Rules

Commas (,)

When to Use Commas:

  • Separate items in a list (apples, oranges, and bananas)
  • After introductory phrases (However, I disagree.)
  • Before conjunctions joining independent clauses (I wanted to go, but I was tired.)
  • Set off non-essential information (My brother, who lives in Paris, is visiting.)
❌ Incorrect (Missing Oxford comma): I invited my parents Oprah Winfrey and the President.
✅ Correct: I invited my parents, Oprah Winfrey, and the President.

Semicolons (;) & Colons (:)

Semicolon: Connects closely related independent clauses or separates complex list items.

✅ Semicolon Examples: I have a big test tomorrow; I can't go out tonight.
We visited Paris, France; London, England; and Rome, Italy.

Colon: Introduces lists, explanations, or quotations.

✅ Colon Examples: You need three things: courage, patience, and practice.
She had one goal: to win the championship.

Apostrophes (')

Two Main Uses:

  • Possession: Sarah's book, the dogs' toys (plural ending in s)
  • Contractions: don't (do not), it's (it is), they're (they are)
❌ Common Mistake: Its a beautiful day. The cats toy is broken.
✅ Correct: It's a beautiful day. (It is)
The cat's toy is broken. (possessive)

Quotation Marks (" ")

Important Rules:

  • Periods and commas go INSIDE quotation marks
  • Colons and semicolons go OUTSIDE quotation marks
  • Question marks depend on context
✅ Examples: She said, "I'll be there soon."
Did he really say "I quit"?
He called her "the best"; however, she disagreed.

Verb Tenses

Present Tenses

Simple Present:

Habits, facts, general truths

✅ Examples: She walks to school every day.
Water boils at 100°C.

Present Continuous:

Actions happening now or temporary situations

✅ Examples: I am studying grammar right now.
They are staying with us this week.

Present Perfect:

Actions started in past, continue to present, or completed at unspecified time

✅ Examples: I have lived here for five years.
She has visited Paris three times.

Past Tenses

Simple Past:

Completed actions at specific time in past

✅ Examples: I walked to school yesterday.
She finished her homework last night.

Past Continuous:

Actions in progress at specific past time

✅ Examples: I was studying when you called.
They were playing soccer at 3 PM.

Past Perfect:

Action completed before another past action

✅ Examples: I had already eaten when she arrived.
They had finished the project before the deadline.

Future Tenses

Simple Future:

Actions that will happen

✅ Examples: I will call you tomorrow.
She is going to study medicine.

Future Perfect:

Action that will be completed before a future time

✅ Examples: By next year, I will have graduated.
They will have finished by 5 PM.

Common Grammar Mistakes

1. Your vs. You're

Remember:

  • Your = possessive (belonging to you)
  • You're = contraction of "you are"
❌ Incorrect: Your going to love this. Is this you're book?
✅ Correct: You're going to love this. Is this your book?

2. Their vs. There vs. They're

Quick Guide:

  • Their = possessive (belonging to them)
  • There = place or existence
  • They're = contraction of "they are"
✅ Examples: Their house is over there, and they're coming soon.

3. Its vs. It's

Test:

If you can replace it with "it is," use it's. Otherwise, use its (possessive).

❌ Incorrect: The dog wagged it's tail. Its raining outside.
✅ Correct: The dog wagged its tail. It's raining outside.

4. Affect vs. Effect

Simple Rule:

  • Affect (usually verb) = to influence
  • Effect (usually noun) = result or consequence
✅ Examples: The weather will affect our plans. (verb)
The effect of the storm was devastating. (noun)

5. Who vs. Whom

Trick:

Replace with he/him: if "he" works, use who; if "him" works, use whom.

✅ Examples: Who called? (He called.)
To whom should I speak? (Should I speak to him?)

6. Less vs. Fewer

Rule:

  • Fewer = countable items (fewer apples, fewer students)
  • Less = uncountable quantities (less water, less time)
❌ Incorrect: I have less friends than you.
✅ Correct: I have fewer friends than you.
