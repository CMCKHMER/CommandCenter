Task 3: Academic Concept

Reading Passage (45 seconds):

Cognitive Dissonance

Cognitive dissonance is a psychological theory that describes the mental discomfort experienced when a person holds two or more contradictory beliefs, values, or attitudes. This discomfort motivates people to reduce the dissonance by changing their beliefs, adding new beliefs, or minimizing the importance of the conflict. The theory suggests that people strive for internal consistency.

Lecture Example:

Professor:

Let me give you an example. Imagine someone who knows smoking causes cancer but continues to smoke. They experience cognitive dissonance. To reduce it, they might quit smoking, or they might convince themselves that the research isn't conclusive, or that living stress-free is more important than avoiding potential health risks. Another example: a student who values honesty but cheats on a test might tell themselves everyone does it or that the test was unfair.

Question:

Explain cognitive dissonance and how the examples from the lecture illustrate this concept.

Task 4: Academic Lecture

Listen to part of a lecture in a biology class:

Professor:

Today we're discussing animal adaptation strategies, specifically camouflage. There are three main types. First, cryptic coloration, where animals blend into their environment like stick insects that look exactly like twigs. Second, disruptive coloration, which breaks up an animal's outline, like the stripes on a tiger that make it hard to see in tall grass. Third, mimicry, where animals imitate other species, like harmless king snakes that have similar color patterns to venomous coral snakes, deterring predators.

Each type serves the same purpose: survival through avoiding detection by predators or prey. Cryptic coloration works best in uniform environments. Disruptive coloration is effective in varied habitats with patterns. Mimicry relies on predators learning to avoid certain appearances through negative experiences with dangerous species.

Question:

Using points and examples from the lecture, describe the three types of camouflage and explain how each helps animals survive.
